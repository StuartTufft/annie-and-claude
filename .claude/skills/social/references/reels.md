# Reels

The owner shoots and edits properly, in Premiere, and enjoys it. So this file
is not about how to cut a video. It is the constraints a shot list has to
respect, and the reasons behind them, so a plan written on Sunday does not
produce footage that fights the platform on Friday.

## The one that matters most

**You cannot reshoot week three.** A puppy is a moving target and every week
looks different from the last. That is why the shot list gets written before
the filming session, not after. The constraint here has never been editing
skill, it is having the right frames in the first place.

Every shot list ends with **"if you only get one shot, get this one."** Dogs do
not do second takes.

## Frame and safe zones

1080x1920, 9:16. Instagram's own interface sits on top of the video, so keep
everything that matters inside a central box of roughly **1010x1280**:

| Edge | Roughly lost to | Keep clear |
|---|---|---|
| Top | Status bar, camera and filter controls | ~150px |
| Bottom | Caption, username, audio strip | ~280px |
| Right | Like / comment / share / more rail | ~90px |

Those are working figures, not gospel. Before committing to any reusable
title-card or lower-third position, check one export on a real handset with the
app open. Handset sizes and app versions move these.

The subject can live anywhere. Text cannot.

## Length

**7 to 30 seconds** is the target for reach. Long enough to tell one thing,
short enough to loop. Past about 3 minutes, Instagram stops recommending a reel
to non-followers at all, which makes length an active choice rather than a
by-product of how much footage exists.

One reel says one thing. If a shot list has two ideas in it, it is two reels.

## Muted first

Most of the audience never turns the sound on. The reel has to work as a silent
piece and be *better* with sound, not dependent on it. That means:

- The story reads from the pictures alone
- On-screen text carries anything the audio would have said
- Sound adds texture and timing, it does not carry information

If the reel is meaningless muted, the shot list is wrong.

## The hook

The first 1.5 seconds decide everything else. It is not a title card and it is
not a slow build. It is the most interesting frame in the whole clip, on screen
immediately, with the question it raises stated or obvious.

A hook that works: something visibly odd or specific, in motion, that makes the
next second necessary. A hook that does not: the dog, sitting, being nice. That
is every other account's opening frame.

## Endings that loop

The last frame should invite the first. A reel that loops cleanly gets watched
twice, and watch time compounds from there. This is a shot-list decision, not
an edit decision: it means planning a final shot that rhymes with the opening
one, so it is available to cut to.

## Audio

Original sound and trending audio are a real trade. Trending audio can carry a
weak reel into more feeds. Original sound is better for anything with the
owner's voice on it, which suits the honest-record and lesson pillars, and it
does not date the post.

Default to original sound. Reach for a track when the reel is pure texture with
nothing to say.

## Originality

Original footage only. The originality score penalises recycled clips, and
anything carrying another platform's watermark is treated as recycled outright.
Never repost something with a TikTok watermark on it.

This is not a constraint here so much as a free advantage: the whole account is
original footage of one specific dog, which is exactly what the ranking wants.

## Write for the save

Ranking signals, strongest first: **saves, shares, DMs, comments, likes.**

"Aww" earns a like. "I needed this" earns a save. The pillars that earn saves
are the honest record and the lessons, because someone in the same week of the
same problem will keep them. Plan for that, not for the cute one.

## What Claude does and does not do here

**Does:** the shot list before filming, deciding which moment of the week is
the reel, the hook, the on-screen text, and afterwards the caption, hashtags,
alt text and any branded still frames.

**Does not:** open the timeline. No transcription, no rough cuts, no EDL or XML
generation. Premiere 2025 already has speech-to-text, auto-captions and
text-based editing built in and does all of it better. The craft stays with the
owner, who is good at it and enjoys it, which is the whole point.
