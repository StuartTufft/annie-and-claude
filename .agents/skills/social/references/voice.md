# Caption voice

Same person as the site, on a smaller page. First person, as the owner. Plain,
warm, specific. Short sentences. The kind of thing someone types on their phone
while the dog is asleep on their foot.

A caption is not a summary of the journal entry. It is one moment from it, told
straight, with the thing that actually happened left in.

## The checklist

Run all of it before calling a caption done.

- [ ] British English
- [ ] First person, as the owner. Not a trainer's case notes.
- [ ] **No em dashes.** Anywhere. Use a comma, a full stop, a colon or
      brackets. En dashes in ranges (21–27 Aug, 20–28kg) are fine.
- [ ] No "X. Not Y, but Z." constructions
- [ ] No glossy summary sentence at the end. Stop when the thought stops.
- [ ] No bold-label list items ("**Day 5** ...")
- [ ] No hashtags buried in the caption body. They go in their own block.
- [ ] Nothing invented. Every fact traces to the source entry.
- [ ] Reads as human-written. Say it out loud. If it sounds like a brand, cut
      it back.

## No AI framing, at all

Stricter than the site rule. The site mentions Claude on About This Project;
Instagram mentions it nowhere. No "written with AI", no model names, no
build-in-public framing, no meta commentary about the process.

This is a deliberate decision (owner, Aug 2026), and the reasoning matters so
it does not get relitigated: the Instagram audience is new puppy owners and
breed people. They do not care that a model helped write the caption, and some
will trust the account less for knowing. That trust is the asset. The
build-in-public story lives on the site, which is linked in the bio, and can be
told properly somewhere that it lands.

If the owner asks for an AI-angle post, that is their call to change. Do not
drift into it on your own.

## Privacy, which is tighter here than on the site

- **Geotag the places, never the house.** The hills, the park, the town, the
  coffee shop: tag them, they are the story. Never geotag the house or the
  street, and never the same doorstep spot on a repeating schedule. The site
  names places freely now (owner decision, Sep 2026); a geotag on the home
  address is still an address.
- No exact address, postcode or street name, in the caption or visible in the
  frame. Check the photo for door numbers, street signs and delivery labels.
- No vet clinic name or location.
- Nothing that maps out when the house is empty. "Back at work tomorrow so
  she's got half a day alone" is the kind of line that reads as honest on a
  journal page and reads as a schedule on a public grid.
- Photos come from the committed, EXIF-stripped copies in `src/journal/`, never
  the Drive originals. That is what keeps location metadata out.

If a draft trips any of these, say so and hold it. Do not publish around it.

## What to keep

The setbacks. The bits that did not work. The retries, the two-minutes-at-a-
time, the rethinks. Every other puppy account posts the highlight. The honest
version is the whole reason this one is worth following, and it is what makes
someone in the same week save the post.

## First comment

One question that invites a real reply, or the site link mention. Keep it to a
line. It exists to start the conversation in the first half hour, which is when
engagement actually counts.
